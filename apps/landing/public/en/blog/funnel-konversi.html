How to Build a Conversion Funnel the Right Way for Indonesian SaaS

May 8, 20267 min readBy the RevKlik Team

Most SaaS founders know their conversion rate. Few know at exactly which step visitors decide to leave. A conversion funnel answers that question, and the answer is often surprising.

In this guide, you will learn how to build a conversion funnel the right way: defining the right steps, measuring drop-off at each stage, and optimizing your biggest bottleneck.

What Is a Conversion Funnel?

A conversion funnel is a visualization of the visitor's path from the start to a specific goal, such as signing up, subscribing, or buying. Each funnel stage represents one step on that path, and each stage has a different dropout rate.

A simple SaaS funnel example:

StageCountDropout
Visits the landing page10.000-
Clicks "Starting at Rp 16.000"2.50075%
Fills in the signup form1.20052%
Completes onboarding60050%
Starts a paid subscription12080%

Out of 10.000 visitors, only 120 subscribe. Overall conversion rate: 1.2%. But the overall number doesn't help you fix anything. What helps is knowing that 75% of visitors don't click the CTA button. That is your biggest bottleneck.

Step 1: Define Your Funnel Goal

Before building a funnel, answer this question: which action is the most valuable to your business?

A few examples:

  • Signup: the visitor registers a free account
  • Activation: the user completes their first setup (creating a project, connecting data, etc.)
  • Paid conversion: the user upgrades to a paid plan
  • Purchase: the visitor completes checkout for a specific product

Each goal needs a different funnel. Don't build one funnel for everything. Build one funnel per main goal.

Step 2: Map Every Step

Write down every step a visitor has to go through from the start to the goal. Be specific: "visits the website" is too general. "Opens the /pricing page from a Google ad" is far more useful.

An example signup funnel for SaaS:

  1. Lands on the homepage (from Google Ads, UTM: utm_source=google)
  2. Clicks "See Pricing"
  3. Opens the pricing page
  4. Clicks "Starting at Rp 16.000" on the Starter plan
  5. Enters email and password
  6. Verifies the email
  7. Completes onboarding (creates the first project)

Each step must be trackable as a separate event in your analytics.

Step 3: Measure Every Stage

Use analytics that can track custom events, not just pageviews. Each step in your funnel needs to be sent as an event with a clear name.

The most common mistake: tracking only pageviews. A pageview doesn't tell you whether the visitor actually interacted with the page or closed it right away.

With RevKlik, you can track custom events with one line of code:

klikapa.track('signup_started', { plan: 'starter' });

Once all events are in place, check the data in the dashboard. You will see the percentage of visitors who move from each step to the next.

Step 4: Identify the Bottleneck

The bottleneck is the stage with the highest dropout rate, where the percentage of visitors who continue is lowest.

Rules of thumb:

  • Dropout below 50% per stage: good enough, focus elsewhere first
  • Dropout 50-70%: there is a problem worth investigating
  • Dropout above 70%: critical bottleneck, prioritize this one

Don't optimize every stage at once. Focus on the biggest bottleneck first. A 10% improvement at the bottleneck will have a bigger impact than a 50% improvement at a stage that is already doing well.

Step 5: Optimize Based on Data

After identifying the bottleneck, form a hypothesis about why visitors drop off and test a fix.

Example hypotheses and fixes:

BottleneckPossible CauseFix to Test
75% don't click the CTA on the homepageThe headline isn't relevantTest a more specific headline
52% drop off at the signup formToo many fieldsReduce fields, defer extra info
50% don't finish onboardingToo many stepsShorten it to 3 steps
80% don't upgrade to paidValue isn't visible during the trialShow locked features earlier

Funnel vs User Journey: What's the Difference?

A funnel is the path you hope visitors follow: a linear path to conversion. A user journey is the path they actually take, including every detour, pages visited repeatedly, and dead ends.

Both matter. The funnel tells you where the problem is. The user journey tells you why, because you can see what visitors did before and after the bottleneck.

For example, if 50% drop off at the pricing page, look at their user journey. Do they go back to the homepage? Do they open the features page first? Do they leave straight away? This is context that a funnel alone doesn't give you.

Common Mistakes When Building a Funnel

  1. Too many steps. A funnel with 15 stages is hard to read and act on. Limit it to 5-7 main steps.
  2. Not tracking custom events. Relying on pageviews alone isn't enough. Interactions such as button clicks and form submits must be tracked separately.
  3. Ignoring traffic sources. The funnel differs for visitors from Google Ads vs Twitter vs email. Segment your funnel by source.
  4. Optimizing the wrong stage. Improving a stage that already converts at 90% has minimal impact. Focus on the bottleneck.
  5. Not measuring over time. Monitor the funnel weekly or monthly. A change in dropout rate can signal a technical problem or a market shift.
