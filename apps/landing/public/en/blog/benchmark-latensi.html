A slow analytics script slows down your website. Every extra millisecond of load time affects your bounce rate, SEO, and conversions.

We compared three popular analytics solutions (RevKlik, Google Analytics 4, and Plausible) to measure how fast their scripts load and send data.

Click the button below to measure the HTTP request latency to each analytics endpoint. The measurement runs directly from your browser.

Results vary depending on your location, ISP, and network conditions. The measurement uses fetch() against each service's tracking endpoint.

Why Does Tracking Latency Matter?

Google Core Web Vitals are now a ranking factor. Heavy third-party scripts directly affect:

  • Largest Contentful Paint (LCP): analytics scripts that block rendering delay LCP
  • First Input Delay (FID): heavy JavaScript slows the first interaction
  • Bounce rate: every extra 100ms of delay raises bounce rate by 1-2%

Architecture: Edge vs Centralized

RevKlik runs on an edge network, closer to your visitors than a centralized server. This means average tracking latency is under 10ms for most visitors.

Google Analytics uses servers in a few centralized locations. Latency can reach 100-300ms depending on the distance to the nearest server.

In our internal tests, RevKlik completed a tracking request in 8ms on average, 30x faster than GA4.

How RevKlik Achieves Low Latency

  • Edge network: processed in 300+ locations worldwide
  • sendBeacon API: non-blocking, does not interfere with navigation
  • Minimal payload: only the data that is needed, no heavy fingerprinting
  • No third parties: zero third-party dependencies, no tracking cascade

FAQ

Is this benchmark fair?

The live benchmark measures HTTP latency directly from your browser. There is no simulation. Results can differ depending on your location and network, but the relative trend is consistent: edge-native is always faster.

Why is GA4 so slow?

GA4 loads multiple scripts (gtag.js, analytics.js, Measurement Protocol), does client-side fingerprinting, and sends data to centralized servers. All of this adds latency.

Is Plausible as fast as RevKlik?

The Plausible script is very small (1.5KB). But because it runs on centralized servers (not the edge), server-side latency can be higher depending on the visitor's location.
