Revenue Attribution: How to Find Out Where Your Money Comes From

April 28, 20268 min readBy the RevKlik Team

The most basic question in business: where does our money come from? "From customers" is not enough of an answer. What you need to know is which marketing channel, which campaign, and which page actually generates revenue.

Most SaaS founders do not know the answer for sure. They may know the overall conversion rate, but cannot say "30% of this month's revenue came from Google ads for keyword X." Without revenue attribution, you waste money on channels that do not work and underinvest in the channels that actually deliver.

What Is Revenue Attribution?

Revenue attribution is the process of linking every dollar of revenue to its source: a specific ad, an email campaign, a social media post, or an organic page. It answers: "If I stop spending money on channel X, how much revenue do I lose?"

Without attribution, you make budget decisions based on guesswork. With attribution, decisions are based on data.

Attribution Models: Which One Is Right?

There are several attribution models you can use. The most common:

ModelHow It WorksWhen to Use It
First-touchAll credit goes to the first interactionMeasuring brand awareness
Last-touchAll credit goes to the last interactionMeasuring direct conversions
LinearCredit is split evenly across all touchpointsA simple, general overview
Time-decayThe closer to the conversion, the more creditLong sales cycles

For most SaaS products, last-touch with UTM context is the most practical starting point. It tells you which page or campaign leads directly to a payment. Once that works, you can add first-touch and time-decay for a fuller picture.

How to Build a Simple Attribution System

You do not need an expensive tool to get started. What you need:

1. Consistent UTM Tracking

Every link that points to your site should have UTM parameters. At a minimum: utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign.

Examples:

  • Google ad: ?utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=analytics-saas
  • Twitter: ?utm_source=twitter&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=launch-announcement
  • Email newsletter: ?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=weekly-digest

Store these UTM parameters in the visitor's session as soon as they land. Do not only track them on the first pageview. Keep them, so that when the visitor converts 3 days later, the UTM is still on record.

2. Connect a Payment Webhook

This is the step most often missed. You cannot attribute revenue from page analytics alone. You need to know when and how much was paid.

The easiest way: connect a webhook from your payment processor (Polar, Xendit) to your analytics system. Every time a payment succeeds, the webhook sends data to analytics, including the customer's email, the amount, and the product.

On the analytics side, link the payment to the visitor's session by email or visitor ID. Now every dollar can be traced back to its source UTM.

3. Build a Simple Report

With linked UTM and payment data, you can build a report like this:

Common Attribution Problems

Dark Social

Not all traffic can be tracked with UTMs. If someone shares your link in a WhatsApp group or a Slack DM without a UTM, it shows up as "direct" in analytics. For B2B SaaS, dark social can be 30-50% of total traffic.

A partial solution: use a unique URL per channel (not just UTMs) and track more specific parameters. And accept that not all attribution will be perfect: 80% accuracy is already very useful.

Multi-Touch Cycles

A visitor might see a Google ad, read a blog post, receive an email, and only subscribe 2 weeks later. The last-touch model records only "email" as the source. The first-touch model records only "Google Ads."

The solution: start with last-touch, then add user journey tracking to see every touchpoint before conversion. RevKlik tracks the full journey, so you can see not only the start and end points but every step in between.

Cross-Device

A visitor finds you on their phone during a commute, but subscribes from a laptop at home. Without a login system, these two devices look like two different visitors.

The solution: for more accurate attribution, connect analytics to the user ID from your authentication system. This links all devices that use the same account.
