How to Choose an Analytics Tool That Complies with Indonesia's PDP Law (Without the Hassle)

May 16, 2026 · 8 min read

Since the enactment of Law Number 27 of 2022 on Personal Data Protection, every business with a website in Indonesia must make sure the way it collects visitor data complies with the regulation. That includes the analytics tools you install on your website.

The problem? Most website owners do not realize that Google Analytics — the most commonly used tool — technically collects personal data such as IP addresses and device fingerprints. Under the PDP Law, this means you need explicit consent from visitors before that data is collected.

In this article, we will cover how to choose analytics tools that are compliant by default with the PDP Law, without the hassle of consent banners, without the risk of fines, and while still getting the insights you need.

The PDP Law and Analytics: What Changed?

Indonesia's PDP Law has principles similar to the GDPR in Europe. Some critical points relevant to analytics:

  • Consent — Every processing of personal data requires clear and specific consent from the data subject (your website visitor).
  • Clear Purpose — Data may only be collected for an explicitly stated purpose. The purpose of analytics is already clear, but you still need transparency.
  • Data Minimization — Only collect data that is truly needed. A full IP address for analytics? Arguably excessive.
  • Data Subject Rights — Visitors have the right to know what data is collected, request access, and request deletion.
Penalties for Violating the PDP Law

Violations of the PDP Law can result in administrative fines of up to 2% of annual revenue, and criminal penalties of fines up to Rp 5 billion and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years. This is not a joke, especially if your visitor data leaks or is processed without a strong legal basis.

Why Can Analytics Violate the PDP Law?

Many people assume analytics is just a "visitor counter." In reality, traditional analytics tools collect far more than that:

Data Collected by Google Analytics

  • IP Address — Technically personal data under the PDP Law (data that can identify an individual directly or indirectly).
  • Cookie ID — A unique identifier that tracks visitors across visits. Clearly personal data.
  • Device Fingerprint — A combination of browser, OS, screen resolution, and more that can identify a user.
  • User Behavior — Every click, scroll, and page visited is sent to Google's servers.

All of this data is sent to Google servers outside Indonesia, which also raises the issue of cross-border data transfer — one more obligation to meet under the PDP Law.

The bottom line: if your analytics uses cookies and collects data that can identify individuals, you must get consent from visitors. If not, you risk violating the PDP Law.

Criteria for PDP Law-Compliant Analytics

Here are the criteria an analytics tool must meet to be compliant by default without needing a consent banner:

  1. No cookies — No cookies, no need for consent. This is the most important foundation.
  2. No personal data collected — IP addresses are anonymized or not collected at all. No user ID that can identify an individual.
  3. Data processed in aggregate — Only stores statistics (number of pageviews, number of unique visitors per period), not per-individual data.
  4. Servers in Indonesia or a safe region — Avoids cross-border data transfer issues.
  5. Transparent about data processing — A clear, easy-to-understand privacy policy.
Key Principle

If your analytics does not collect personal data at all, then the PDP Law does not apply to that analytics activity. Cookieless analytics that only counts aggregates is legally outside the scope of the PDP Law. This means: no consent banner, no privacy impact assessment, and no risk of violations.

How to Implement PDP Law-Compliant Analytics

Step 1: Audit Your Current Analytics

Check whether your website uses Google Analytics, Facebook Pixel, or other tracking tools. Open your website's source code or use a browser extension such as Ghostery or Blacklight to detect trackers.

Step 2: Choose a Cookieless Tool

Choose an analytics tool that does not use cookies and does not collect personal data. As explained in the article on cookieless analytics, this approach removes the need for a consent banner while ensuring regulatory compliance.

Step 3: Install the Tracking Script

With RevKlik, the process is very simple. You just add one line of script to your website:

<script defer data-site-id="your-site-id" src="https://app.revklik.com/klikapa.js"></script>

This script is about 4 KB (gzip) — compared to Google Analytics, which can reach 45KB. This means your website stays fast and there is no negative impact on Core Web Vitals.

Step 4: Remove the Consent Banner (If It Was Only for Analytics)

If your consent banner exists only because of analytics, you can remove it after migrating to cookieless analytics. Congratulations, your website is now cleaner and more professional.

Step 5: Update Your Privacy Policy

Even though cookieless analytics does not require consent, it is still recommended to mention in your privacy policy that you use analytics that does not collect personal data. This shows good faith and transparency.

Important Note on Consent Banners

Many websites in Indonesia add a consent banner "because of Google Analytics" without really understanding the implications. A consent banner that is not implemented properly (for example, auto-accepting after 5 seconds) can actually be seen as proof that you knew there was a compliance problem but did not take it seriously. With cookieless analytics, you remove this problem at the root.
